The Childhood

Zviad Gamsakhurdia grew up in the home of Konstantine Gamsakhurdia, one of Georgia’s most celebrated writers and intellectuals. It was a household shaped by literature, history, serious thought, and a profound attachment to Georgia. From an early age, Zviad was surrounded by books, conversation, and a strong awareness of his country’s cultural heritage, its struggles, and its aspirations.
In their family, love for Georgia meant knowing the country’s history, protecting its language and culture, respecting the sacrifices of earlier generations, and remaining faithful to its dignity even in difficult times. Patriotism was therefore part of everyday life, expressed through reading, discussion, memory, and personal example.
These early influences later became visible in many parts of his life: in his scholarly work, his defence of Georgian culture, his public activity, and his political struggle for the country’s freedom and independence.

Family Life

Introductory text about Zviad Gamsakhurdia’s private and family life.

Wife

Zviad Gamsakhurdia was married twice. His first wife was Dali Lolua, with whom he had a son, Konstantine. His second wife, Manana Archvadze-Gamsakhurdia, became the first First Lady of independent Georgia; together, they had two sons, Tsotne and Giorgi.

Children

Zviad Gamsakhurdia had three sons. His eldest son, Konstantine, was born during his marriage to Dali Lolua, while Tsotne and Giorgi were his sons with Manana Archvadze-Gamsakhurdia. Their family history was deeply affected by Georgia’s political upheaval, including their father’s removal from power, exile and death, and all three sons later participated in efforts to preserve his legacy and clarify the circumstances of his death.
