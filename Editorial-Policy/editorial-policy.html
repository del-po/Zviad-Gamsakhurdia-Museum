Purpose

A historical record should remain open, traceable and correctable.

The museum presents biographical, political, literary and archival material concerning Zviad Gamsakhurdia and the historical period in which he lived. Because many subjects remain contested, incomplete or dependent upon personal recollection, the museum applies editorial standards intended to make distinctions between different kinds of evidence clear.

01

Evidence

Documented Facts

Factual claims should, whenever possible, be supported by identifiable documents, contemporary records, publications, photographs, recordings or other traceable sources.

02

Attribution

Memory Is a Source, Not a Substitute for Evidence

Personal recollections, interviews and family testimony may provide important historical information, but they should remain clearly attributed to the person providing the account.

03

Interpretation

Fact and Interpretation Must Remain Distinct

Editorial interpretation, scholarly analysis and political judgment should not be presented as though they were themselves primary historical facts.

04

Disagreement

Contested History Should Be Identified as Contested

Where credible sources disagree, the museum should identify the disagreement, attribute competing accounts and avoid concealing uncertainty behind a single definitive formulation.

05

Corrections

The Record Must Remain Correctable

New documents, better sourcing or demonstrated errors may require previously published material to be corrected, clarified or expanded.

06

Contributions

Submitted Materials Require Provenance

Documents, photographs, recordings and other contributed materials should be accompanied, whenever possible, by information explaining their origin, ownership, date, people represented and circumstances of preservation.

07

Private Material

Publication Requires Responsibility and Consent

Private correspondence, family materials and information concerning living persons should be handled with appropriate consideration for consent, privacy, ownership and publication rights.

08

Transparency

Readers Should Be Able to Follow the Record

Wherever practical, the museum should identify sources, provenance, dates, authorship and verification status so that readers can understand how a historical claim was constructed.
