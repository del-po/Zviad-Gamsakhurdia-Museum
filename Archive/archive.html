How to read the archive

A digital object is only as reliable as its description.

01

Provenance

Every record should name the holding institution, donor, collection, accession history or known chain of custody.

02

Date & Certainty

Exact dates, approximate dates and later attributions should be visibly distinguished rather than merged into one chronology.

03

Authenticity & Version

Originals, certified copies, drafts, reproductions, published versions and disputed items require different labels.

04

Rights & Access

Copyright, privacy, personal data, donor restrictions and source-institution terms determine what may be displayed.

05

Transcription & Translation

Searchable text should remain linked to the original image, with editorial insertions and uncertain readings identified.

06

Context & Disagreement

A record page should explain who created an item, why, for whom and how later sources agree or conflict with it.

Catalogue language

What the access labels mean.

The archive can publish a useful record even when the underlying object cannot yet be displayed. Clear labels prevent an absent scan from being mistaken for an absent source.

Verified reference

Identity and external holding reference confirmed.

Digitized selection

One or more digital objects are available.

Metadata record

Description exists; image or file is not yet published.

Under review

Provenance, date, description or rights require further work.

Rights restricted

Access is limited by copyright, privacy or donor terms.

Sensitive history

Material requires contextual and trauma-aware presentation.
