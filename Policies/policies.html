Policy 01

E

How the museum selects, verifies, explains, attributes and corrects historical content.

Purpose and scope Evidence and verification Contested history Corrections Contributions and rights

Editorial Policy

History should remain traceable to evidence.

The museum combines public history, biography, archival description and research interpretation. Its editorial work aims to distinguish documented fact from interpretation, testimony, allegation, uncertainty and commemoration.

E—01

Purpose and scope

The editorial policy applies to biographical texts, timelines, catalogue records, translations, captions, essays, blog entries, metadata, audiovisual descriptions and contributed materials published by the project.

The museum is not a substitute for the archives, libraries, publishers or private custodians that hold original materials. Where possible, records identify the original source, custodian, publication or collection.

E—02

Evidence, sourcing and verification

Important factual claims should be supported by identifiable sources. Preference is given to primary documents, verified editions, archival catalogues, official records, contemporaneous publications and reputable scholarship.

Source visibility

Readers should be able to understand where a claim or item comes from.

Date clarity

Exact, approximate, inferred and disputed dates should not be presented as equivalent.

Attribution

Interpretations, recollections and allegations are attributed to their speakers or authors.

Version control

Substantial revisions should record what changed and when.

E—03

Contested events and political history

The museum covers periods in which terminology, legal characterization, responsibility and historical meaning are disputed. It should not erase disagreement by presenting one contested interpretation as an uncontested fact.

  • Established dates and events are separated from disputed explanations.
  • Material viewpoints are attributed and placed in historical context.
  • Loaded terminology is quoted, explained or replaced with precise description.
  • Absence of evidence is not presented as proof that an event did or did not occur.
  • Commemorative purpose does not eliminate the obligation to identify uncertainty.
E—04

Corrections, challenges and right of reply

Readers may challenge a date, identification, quotation, translation, attribution or interpretation through the Contact page. A useful correction identifies the relevant page and supplies supporting evidence or a precise reason for review.

Minor spelling or formatting changes may be made silently. Material corrections should be recorded in the page or project change log. A correction does not necessarily mean deleting the earlier record; where historically useful, the change and reason may be preserved.

E—05

Contributed material, copyright and editorial independence

Submission does not guarantee publication. Contributors should identify ownership, provenance, known restrictions, requested credit and any privacy concern. The project may request further evidence, decline material or limit access.

Copyright remains with the relevant rightsholder unless a written agreement states otherwise. Publication permission does not transfer ownership. The museum may remove or restrict material while a good-faith rights, privacy or provenance concern is reviewed.

Donors, partners, sponsors, public bodies and family members may supply knowledge and materials, but they do not receive an automatic right to dictate historical conclusions or prevent good-faith correction and contextualization.

Use of translation, transcription and AI-assisted tools

Translations and transcriptions should identify the language, source and responsible editor where practical. Automated transcription, translation, search or drafting tools may be used to assist work, but consequential historical claims require human review against the underlying source.

Synthetic reconstructions, colorization, restoration or generated imagery must be clearly labelled so that readers do not mistake them for unaltered historical records.

Privacy Policy

Collect only what the project needs.

This notice describes the handling of personal information received through the website, project correspondence, submissions and ordinary technical operation. It should be updated whenever analytics, mailing, payment or other external services are introduced.

Implementation note

Before launch, confirm the project’s formal data-controller identity, contact address, hosting provider, processors, retention schedule and any analytics or cookie tools actually used. This page should describe the deployed system, not a hypothetical one.

P—01

Controller and scope

The Zviad Gamsakhurdia Digital Museum is an independent digital project initiated and developed by Gocha Okreshidze / AI Law LLC. Privacy questions and requests may be submitted through the project Contact page under “Rights & Privacy.”

The notice applies to website visitors, correspondents, contributors, identifiable persons represented in submitted material, and individuals who request correction, access, restriction or removal.

P—02

Information the project may collect

01
Contact information

Name, email address, organization, location and preferred language when voluntarily supplied.

02
Message and submission content

Questions, corrections, source links, provenance details, files, rights information and correspondence.

03
Technical information

Basic server logs such as IP address, time, requested page, browser or error information, where generated by hosting systems.

04
Historical personal data

Names, images, voices, relationships, correspondence and biographical information contained in archival materials.

P—03

Why information is used

Depending on the circumstances, information may be used to:

  • respond to inquiries and manage correspondence;
  • assess, verify, catalogue and publish contributed material;
  • correct records and investigate rights or privacy concerns;
  • protect the security, integrity and availability of the website;
  • document permission, provenance, attribution and access restrictions;
  • carry out historical, journalistic, archival, scholarly and educational work.

Processing may rely, as applicable, on consent, steps taken at the individual’s request, legal obligations, legitimate interests, protection of rights, and lawful archival, historical, journalistic or research purposes. The proper basis depends on the actual activity and applicable law.

P—04

Retention, disclosure and international services

Ordinary inquiries should be kept only as long as reasonably needed to answer, document and protect the correspondence. Records connected to published archive items, permission, provenance, correction history or legal claims may need to be retained for a longer period as part of the project record.

Information may be handled by service providers necessary for hosting, security, email, cloud storage or technical support. It may also be shared when required by law, to protect rights and safety, or with the contributor’s authorization. The museum does not sell personal information.

Internet and cloud services may process information in more than one country. Before launch, the project should identify its actual processors and ensure that any required safeguards for international transfers are in place.

P—05

Access, correction, objection and removal requests

Subject to applicable law and legitimate archival, historical, freedom-of-expression and legal-record interests, an individual may request information about processing, access to personal data, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, withdrawal of consent or a copy in an available format.

A request should identify the person, relevant page or material, the requested action and enough information to assess identity and the request. The museum may ask for additional verification and may preserve a limited record of the request and decision.

Cookies

Essential storage may be used for security or basic functionality. Non-essential analytics or marketing cookies should not be activated without an appropriate notice and, where required, consent.

Children

The website is educational but is not intended to solicit personal data from children. A parent or guardian may contact the project about information submitted by a minor.

Security

Reasonable technical and organizational safeguards should be used, but no internet transmission or storage system can be represented as completely risk-free.

Policy changes

Material changes should update the version date and, where appropriate, include a summary of the change.

Historical archives and living persons

Archival records may contain information about living people. Publication decisions should consider public interest, historical value, sensitivity, age of the material, expectations of privacy, accuracy, potential harm, consent, copyright and lawful archival or expressive purposes.

The existence of a document does not automatically require unrestricted online publication. Redaction, description-only access, delayed access or restricted access may be more appropriate in some cases.

Accessibility Statement

Access is part of preservation.

A digital museum cannot fulfil its public purpose if readers cannot navigate, perceive or understand it. Accessibility is an ongoing design, editorial and testing responsibility rather than a one-time declaration.

A—01

Commitment and technical target

The project aims to follow the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA as the principal technical benchmark. This is a target and continuing work programme, not a claim that every current page, historical file or third-party resource already conforms.

STANDARD WCAG 2.2 LEVEL AA TARGET
A—02

Features incorporated into the website

01 Keyboard navigation

Interactive controls should be reachable and usable without a mouse.

02 Visible focus

Links and controls should show a clear focus indicator.

03 Semantic structure

Headings, landmarks, lists, labels and native controls communicate page organization.

04 Reduced motion

Animations respond to the user’s reduced-motion preference where implemented.

05 Responsive reflow

Layouts adapt across screen sizes without requiring one fixed viewport.

06 Text alternatives

Meaningful images should receive useful alternative text or nearby description.

A—03

Historical images, documents, audio and video

Archival content creates particular accessibility challenges. The project’s long-term objective is to provide, where appropriate and lawful:

  • descriptive alternative text for meaningful images;
  • captions, transcripts or summaries for audio and video;
  • OCR text or human transcription for scanned documents;
  • language identification and translations where available;
  • clear notice when a file is inaccessible, incomplete or pending remediation;
  • an alternative way to request information contained in a difficult format.
A—04

Known and likely limitations

The collection may include handwritten manuscripts, damaged recordings, low-contrast scans, complex historical layouts, material in Georgian and other languages, and third-party files whose accessibility the project does not control.

Three-dimensional and scroll-based decorative scenes may be difficult for some users. Essential information should not depend solely on animation, color, pointer movement or visual position. Reduced-motion and text-based alternatives should be maintained and improved.

A—05

Accessibility feedback and assistance

Readers may report a barrier, request content in another available format, or explain what assistive technology and page were involved. Reports should be prioritized according to severity, frequency, public importance and available remediation options.

Please include the page address, the affected content or control, what happened, and the browser or assistive technology used where comfortable doing so.

Continuing work

Accessibility roadmap

  1. 01
    Audit core templates

    Test navigation, forms, focus order, zoom, contrast and reduced motion.

  2. 02
    Improve archive records

    Add alt text, transcripts, captions and document descriptions as records are reviewed.

  3. 03
    Test with real users

    Include keyboard, screen-reader, low-vision and cognitive-accessibility perspectives.

  4. 04
    Publish known issues

    Record significant unresolved barriers and remediation progress.

Formal conformance status

Unless a dated independent or internal audit is published, this statement should be read as a commitment and target—not a formal claim of full WCAG conformance. Formal conformance requires evaluation of the complete pages and processes in scope.
