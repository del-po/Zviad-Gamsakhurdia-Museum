A Museum Without Walls.

The mission

Preserve the record. Explain the context. Keep inquiry open.

The museum brings together biography, scholarship, political history and archival evidence in one expandable digital space. Its purpose is not merely to commemorate, but to help readers examine how a private life, intellectual work and public history intersect.

M—01
Historical memory is strongest when readers can move from a claim to its source, from an image to its date, and from a public event to the different voices that recorded it.

The long-term objective is a reliable bilingual research and educational platform containing contextual essays, verified records, digitized materials, publication histories, testimony and clearly identified areas of uncertainty.

Why a digital museum?

Because the historical record is dispersed.

01

Materials live in different places.

Books, manuscripts, state records, private photographs, recordings and press materials are held by different archives, libraries, families and individuals.

02

Access should not depend on geography.

A digital platform allows students, researchers and the public to encounter material even when they cannot visit the physical institution that preserves it.

New documents, corrections, translations and testimonies can be added without treating the museum as a finished or closed account.

How the museum works

From discovery to a public record.

A digital item should not be published as an isolated image. It should travel through a documented process that preserves what is known, what is inferred and what remains uncertain.

?
01 / Discovery

Identify a document, image, recording or reference.

The process may begin with an archival catalogue, a published source, a family collection, a reader submission or a reference found in another document.

CHECKED
02 / Verification

Check identity, date, source and authenticity.

Metadata is compared with catalogues, editions, internal evidence and, where available, the knowledge of custodians or people connected to the material.

CONTEXT
03 / Context

Explain where the item belongs in the larger history.

A record is connected to people, events, works, places and competing interpretations so that it does not appear as self-explanatory evidence.

3×
04 / Preservation

Retain files, metadata, rights information and provenance.

Digital preservation requires more than a visible webpage: stable file copies, clear naming, source history, usage conditions and records of later corrections.

PUBLIC
05 / Publication

Make the material usable, attributable and correctable.

Published records should identify their sources, explain access limitations and provide a route for corrections or additional information.

Editorial principles

Respect for evidence and respect for readers.

Read the full editorial policy →
01

Source transparency

Readers should be able to understand where information comes from and whether it is based on an original record, published research, testimony or editorial inference.

02

Clear attribution

Opinions, accusations and interpretations are attributed to their authors rather than presented as unqualified fact.

03

Visible uncertainty

Unknown dates, disputed identities, incomplete provenance and conflicting accounts are identified rather than silently resolved.

04

Corrections remain possible

The museum is designed to receive evidence-based corrections and to update records when stronger information becomes available.

05

Rights and privacy

Private correspondence, family material and sensitive testimony require attention to copyright, consent, dignity and legitimate access restrictions.

06

Accessible public history

Scholarly care should coexist with readable language, responsive design, bilingual access and support for different ways of navigating the collection.

INDEPENDENT

Project position

Independent, public-facing and open to collaboration.

The Zviad Gamsakhurdia Digital Museum is an independent digital project. It is not presented as a state archive, government body or substitute for the institutions and private custodians that preserve original materials.

The museum seeks cooperation with archives, libraries, researchers, family members, witnesses, translators and readers. Attribution and links to the original custodian should remain visible wherever possible.
