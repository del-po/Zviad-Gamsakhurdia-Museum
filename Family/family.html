Chapter one

Raised in Konstantine Gamsakhurdia’s Household

Zviad Gamsakhurdia was born in Tbilisi in 1939 to the writer and academician Konstantine Gamsakhurdia and Miranda Palavandishvili. His childhood unfolded inside a household closely connected with Georgian literature, historical memory, and the preservation of national culture under Soviet rule.

02 1940s—1957

Chapter two

School Years and the First Political Choices

Gamsakhurdia attended Tbilisi Secondary School No. 47 and graduated in 1957. His school years were not only academic: by the mid-1950s he and other young people were organizing, writing, and distributing anti-Soviet proclamations connected with the idea of Georgian independence.

01

First marriage

Konstantine Gamsakhurdia

The eldest son of Zviad Gamsakhurdia and namesake of his celebrated grandfather, Konstantine is a historian, Iranist, translator, and public figure. Since 2024, he has served as General Director of the National Parliamentary Library of Georgia, combining scholarship with the preservation of Georgia’s literary and historical heritage.

Explore Konstantine’s Family Archive ↗
02

Second marriage

Tsotne Gamsakhurdia

A son of Zviad Gamsakhurdia and Manana Archvadze-Gamsakhurdia, Tsotne is a political scientist and public figure. His public activity has remained closely connected with Georgia’s recent political history and efforts to preserve and clarify the legacy of his father.

Explore Tsotne’s Life and Records ↗
03

Second marriage

Giorgi Gamsakhurdia

The youngest son of Zviad Gamsakhurdia and Manana Archvadze-Gamsakhurdia, Giorgi pursued a career in public administration and national security. He served as a deputy minister responsible for displaced persons and refugees and later as Georgia’s police attaché in Poland, working in international law-enforcement cooperation.
